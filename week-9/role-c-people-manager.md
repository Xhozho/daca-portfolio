# Week 9 — Role C: People Manager

**Project:** UrbanStyle.ltd — Data Analyst Hiring Guide  
**Role:** C — People Manager

## 1. What I Would Look for in a Candidate

As a People Manager, I believe technical skills matter, but so does how someone communicates, solves problems and works with others. These are the qualities I would look for when hiring a data analyst at UrbanStyle.

**Communication:** A good analyst should be able to explain findings clearly, especially to people without a technical background. They should listen carefully, respond to questions and make sure their message is understood.

**Problem-solving:** I would value someone who investigates when something doesn't look right rather than ignoring it. For example, if UrbanStyle's sales report showed unexpected figures, a good analyst would check the data, investigate possible causes and explain their findings before recommending a solution.

**Teamwork:** A good teammate communicates progress, shares knowledge and offers support when needed. Being sociable and approachable can help people work together, but someone doesn't need to be outgoing to contribute positively to a team.

**Cultural fit:** I would look for someone who respects different perspectives, accepts constructive feedback and is willing to improve. Nobody knows everything, and recognising mistakes is part of learning. Candidates should also feel comfortable expressing respectful disagreements when they have a valid reason.

## 2. Three Green Flags

1. **Open-mindedness:** Accepts feedback, reflects on mistakes and demonstrates improvement.
2. **Clear communication:** Listens carefully and explains ideas in an understandable way.
3. **Supportive teamwork:** Shares information, communicates challenges and helps teammates work towards shared goals.

## 3. Two Red Flags

1. **Poor communication of concerns:** Repeatedly avoids raising important problems or explaining decisions, even when given opportunities to clarify.
2. **Unwillingness to learn:** Dismisses feedback without consideration or refuses to reconsider an approach when presented with evidence.

## 4. Practical Interview Question

**"Can you tell me about a time you disagreed with feedback from a manager or teammate? How did you handle it?"**

I would look at how the candidate explains the disagreement, whether they considered the other person's perspective and what they learned.

Disagreeing with a manager isn't automatically a red flag. What matters is whether the candidate communicated respectfully and considered the evidence.

## 5. Recommendation to Liis

I recommend that UrbanStyle uses the same behaviour-based interview questions for every candidate and assesses their answers using clear criteria.

Rather than judging candidates by confidence or personality alone, interviewers should look for real examples of communication, problem-solving, teamwork and willingness to learn.

A strong junior analyst doesn't need to know everything from the beginning, but they should be willing to ask questions, take responsibility and improve.
